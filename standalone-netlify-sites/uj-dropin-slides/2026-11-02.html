Good news: the AI economics and governance stream is funded

Lightcone Commons has awarded us a grant (announced 1 Nov) for a 12-month stream of evaluations of research on AI's economic effects and governance. The case for it is in our EA Forum post (20 Oct, with David Manheim):

  • Economists and policy analysts are producing influential work on AI growth, labour, chip smuggling and regulation, and it is already shaping fiscal scenarios, policy commentary and legislation.
  • Little of it has had careful, public, expert scrutiny: seminar critiques are private, blog responses are self-selected, and journal review takes years.
  • We aim for public credibility signals within about a month of commissioning, with quantified ratings, claim-level assessment and author responses.
  • We have briefs on 34 candidate papers, and we want your input on which matter most. We can go beyond those, of course: suggestions welcome.

"We hope to provide more rigor without accepting the (far longer) timelines for traditional review."

Planned: part-time research lead, AI economics and governance

  • Purpose: run our 12-month stream, jointly with David Reinstein and others on the team, evaluating research on the economics and governance of AI: labour markets, growth, concentration of wealth and power, regulation, state capacity.
  • Main work: find and prioritize the most useful research; commission expert evaluators and oversee evaluations; turn open debates into Pivotal Questions, where better evidence could change decisions.
  • Also: run workshops with researchers, practitioners and funders that map disagreements and record how views shift; write short, timely syntheses for research users; work with our field specialists and advisors.
  • Time: about half-time for about 12 months, remote.
  • Background: research experience in economics, quantitative social science or policy; familiarity with AI economics or governance; comfortable judging research quality and managing several people and deadlines; good communication skills.

Interested, or know someone? Email contact@unjournal.org or use our expression-of-interest form. Details are still being finalised; a full description will follow.

More detail

The stream plans about 15 evaluation packages and two Pivotal Questions, each with a workshop. Applied AI work goes out of date quickly, so the job is getting careful public evaluation out fast, well within journal timelines. The lead would work closely with the co-directors and our field specialist team.

Don't promise a hire or give pay: the hire depends on finding the right person. No amounts.

Open discussion

Questions, suggestions, challenges: Zoom chat or just speak up.

Some starting questions

  • What would make a strong researcher take on a fast AI economics evaluation? Purpose, recognition, prizes, pay?
  • How should evaluators use AI? Our current policy is evolving: since we want fast, high-quality turnaround on AI-relevant work, we may encourage AI use more. Should we run strong AI evaluations first and ask humans to check them, or keep them separate and compare? Examples of AI review workspaces for our first two papers (AI-generated, not checked by a person, not Unjournal evaluations): Forecasting the Economic Effects of AI · Economic Scenarios for Transformative AI. We'd also consider trialling refine.ink.
  • AI governance work goes stale in weeks. How fast do we need to be, and what formats should we evaluate (dashboards, datasets, living reviews)?

We'll respond to every comment, including those we don't reach live, and add them to the record.

On the second question: our AI-vs-human comparison (60 papers) found the model caught about 80% of issues human evaluators agreed on: results. Gary King's recent "peerless" work is close to our planned rater study.
