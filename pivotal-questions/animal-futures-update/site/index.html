What the forecasts suggest

These are current crowd estimates for precisely worded questions. One outcome has resolved; the rest are judgments that can change. Each claim links to its question and resolution rule.

Confirmed outcome

A cultivated meat approval arrived

A new-product approval question resolved Yes on August 4 after Aleph Farms’ Singapore approval was identified. This is a regulatory milestone. GFI says actual availability remains limited by geography and production volume.

Current forecast

Plant-based meat: no rapid growth expected

The crowd gives 2% to plant-based meat and seafood reaching 10% of US retail packaged-meat dollar sales before 2031, unchanged since October 3. The 2025 share was 1.4%, so this would mean roughly a sevenfold increase.

Current forecast

Policy can move in different directions

On the question’s stated definition, forecasters put about 80% combined probability on at least one further US state cultivated-meat ban before July 2027. They give 3% to the Save Our Bacon Act becoming law in that period.

Current forecast

In-ovo sexing: a large EU–US gap

The crowd estimates 43.6% of EU commercial layers and 2.8% of the US hen flock will be sexed in ovo in Q1 2027. The question definitions and data sources matter for this comparison; see the discussion below.

Other forecasts worth watching
Octopus farming.

The crowd gives 1% to approval of Nueva Pescanova’s planned commercial octopus farm in the Canary Islands before June 2027. From an animal welfare standpoint, approval would be a setback, so this is a forecast that a bad outcome is very unlikely in the near term.

Corporate commitments.

The crowd estimates 11.3% cage-free and 4.88% broiler commitment fulfillment under the questions’ Chicken Watch definitions. These questions now have 22 and 20 forecasters.

Shrimp welfare.

The crowd estimates 10.5 of Europe’s top 20 grocery retailers will have public shrimp welfare commitments before July 2027. A separate question estimates 8.07 billion shrimp per year covered by SWP Humane Slaughter Initiative agreements.

AI and animals.

The 2034 AI-lab guidelines question asks how many of seven AI companies (Alibaba, Anthropic, DeepSeek, Google, Meta, OpenAI and xAI) will, by the end of 2034, explicitly name animal welfare as a consideration in the instructions used to train their language models. That covers documents used directly in training, such as Anthropic’s constitution, and guidelines given to the human raters whose judgments train the model, such as OpenAI’s Model Spec. Two and three companies are tied as the most likely answers (22% each), and the crowd spreads probability across several outcomes. This is a long-horizon indicator of whether AI systems will be shaped to weigh animal interests, rather than a near-term welfare outcome.

For decisions: a regulatory approval, a policy pledge, and animal impact are different steps. These forecasts are useful for deciding what evidence to collect next: commercialization and adoption after approval; implementation after pledges; and why uptake differs across places. They do not yet rank interventions by welfare impact.

What the discussion adds

There are 35 comments across the 21 question cards; eight questions have none. A new comment, checked against the benchmark’s own page, raises a question about whether one AI-welfare outcome still measures what the tournament intends.

Is ANIMA still the right benchmark?

A new comment notes the benchmark page calls ANIMA first-generation/legacy for distinguishing frontier models. The benchmark team’s page says it remains useful for open-source training and points to TAC for frontier comparisons. The question covers models through June 2027, so organizers should check score coverage and whether the measure still fits its purpose.

When does a ban count?

A forecaster flagged Louisiana’s signed “trigger law”, which depends on a future Supreme Court ruling. Metaculus clarified that it counts only once the condition is met. A signed bill need not be an operative restriction.

Which hens are counted?

A commenter found a revised historical EU estimate and a difference between commercial-layer and broader measures. The criteria were clarified to use commercial layers and the first 2027 report.

One date mismatch caught early

On the new cage-free law question, a commenter flagged conflicting cutoff dates. A follow-up noted the mismatch, and a later reply said the fine print had been changed. It now agrees with the July 1, 2027 cutoff in the title and criteria.

Two more comments with practical implications

Forecasters pressed for a precise approval definition before the cultivated-meat question resolved. The eventual Yes is a specific product and jurisdiction milestone, not evidence of broad market access.

On the 2040 aquaculture question, a participant flagged an older OECD-FAO background dataset and linked newer figures. Check this baseline before using the forecast in a funding or policy brief.

Suggestions for the tournament

These are proposals for the organizers and participants, based on this snapshot. They are open for comment.

  1. Bring the new questions to the people who can use them. The second wave now has 19–27 forecasters per question. Invite people with corporate-campaign, shrimp-welfare, and retail-market knowledge, and ask them for the evidence driving their estimate.
  2. Ask what would change a decision. For each topic, seek a short response from a funder, advocate, or policymaker: which threshold would change a grant, campaign, or policy position? A forecast has more value when that decision is named.
  3. Keep dates and definitions consistent. The cage-free question discussion caught a mismatch between the event cutoff and fine print. The Louisiana law and EU in-ovo examples also show why trigger conditions, denominators, and data revisions need early clarification.
  4. Check the AI outcome’s coverage. The ANIMA page now describes its frontier-model comparison as legacy while retaining open-source training value. Confirm that scores will be available through the question window and that this is still the intended outcome.
  5. Track participation and decision use. Separate per-question coverage from unique human participants, activity, revisions, and comments. No documented use of a forecast in a funding or policy decision surfaced in the public sources checked for this update; asking decision-makers directly should be a priority.

The tournament rules say three longer-horizon questions are excluded from leaderboard calculations, while prize eligibility requires active forecasts on at least two of them for most of the tournament. Explain this prominently when recruiting participants.
