Animal Futures: what the forecasts say so far

Checked October 13, 2026 · Markdown version for sharing

Feedback welcome: Use the Hypothes.is sidebar at the top right to annotate this report. To comment on the dashboard, open it and use the sidebar there. Corrections, missing sources, and suggestions are useful.

The Animal Futures tournament lists 21 questions. The original 16 now have 48–71 forecasters each (mean 54). Five added on September 20 have 19–27 each (mean 23), up from 15–23 on October 3. These are per-question counts, not unique participants.

One question has resolved: a new cultivated-meat product received regulatory approval following Aleph Farms’ Singapore approval. That is a milestone; commercial availability remains limited.

Current forecasts put 2% on plant-based meat and seafood reaching 10% of US packaged-meat sales before 2031; the 2025 share was 1.4%. Separately, they put 1% on approval of Nueva Pescanova’s planned commercial octopus farm before June 2027, an outcome that would be a setback for animal welfare. The crowd puts about 80% on at least one more US state cultivated-meat ban by July 2027, under the question’s definition. For Q1 2027, the estimates are 43.6% for EU commercial layers and 2.8% for the US hen flock to be sexed in ovo.

A new discussion raises a useful design question. The comment on the ANIMA forecast notes that the benchmark’s own page now calls it legacy for distinguishing frontier models, while retaining value for open-source training. Organizers could check whether the forecast’s outcome will remain measurable through its full window and still matches the intended decision.

There are 35 comments across the 21 question cards; eight questions have none. For funders, advocates, and policymakers, these forecasts can point to evidence worth gathering next, such as adoption after approval and implementation after company pledges. They do not rank interventions by welfare impact. I found no publicly documented funding or policy decision citing a tournament forecast in the sources checked; only one question has resolved, so it is too early to judge accuracy.
